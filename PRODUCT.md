# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is prospective high-school students across the Bay Area who are curious about public and global health — often at the very start, with a first spark of interest or a half-formed idea, and frequently with little prior exposure to programs like this. Many arrive unsure whether something this "Stanford" is meant for them. Secondary audiences are parents and families weighing whether to encourage a student to join, and partner schools, clinics, and community organizations that might refer students or collaborate. A separate team-only `/admin` dashboard serves the Stanford undergraduate organizers; it is a product surface and sits outside this brand register.

## Product Purpose

YPHA is a year-long, cohort-based accelerator that walks high schoolers from a first spark of interest to a finished public-health project — a research paper or poster, a community event, a school-club initiative, or a nonprofit they found. The public site exists to make that path feel real and reachable, and to convert interested students into cohort applicants. Success is a strong, diverse applicant pool — especially students from under-resourced backgrounds — who understand what the program offers and believe they belong in it.

## Positioning

A YC-style accelerator for high-school public-health projects: real mentorship and a structured year that turn curiosity into work a student can carry into the world.

## Conversion & proof

The primary CTA is to apply to the cohort. The secondary fallback, for a visitor not yet ready to apply, is to attend the Leadership Summit — the low-commitment on-ramp where many students take their first step.

The line a visitor should remember after ten seconds: this is a real accelerator that will walk me from an idea to a finished public-health project, and I belong here.

The belief ladder a visitor climbs before applying: first, that public health is something they could actually do; then, that the program meets them where they are, with no prior experience required; then, that it is credible — Stanford-run, with real mentors and a real year-long structure; then, that students like them have done real work here; and finally, that they can apply now.

Proof on hand today is the Stanford affiliation and founding organizations (the Stanford Undergraduate Global Health Club, the Stanford Journal of Public Health, and the Stanford Center for Innovation in Global Health), the Leadership Summit first held in April 2026, and the concrete program structure and timeline. Student testimonials and featured symposium projects do not exist yet — this is the first cohort — and should be added to the site as they accrue.

## Brand Personality

Warm and human: plainspoken, encouraging, never corporate or buzzwordy. Rigorous and credible: academically grounded and Stanford-caliber, evidence-driven without being dry. Ambitious and energizing: a launchpad that takes teenagers' ideas seriously. Equity runs underneath all of it — the voice should make a student who assumed "programs like this aren't for me" feel explicitly invited.

## Anti-references

Not generic SaaS or startup marketing — no gradient-drenched heroes, hero-metric templates, identical icon-card grids, or buzzword copy. Not a stuffy institutional university-department page — dense, formal, and lifeless. Not childish or juvenile — the audience is teenagers, addressed as capable near-adults rather than talked down to.

## Design Principles

Meet students where they start — lower the barrier at every step and assume curiosity, not credentials. Show the path, not just the pitch — let the concrete structure (mentorship, labs, the year-long timeline, the symposium) do the persuading. Treat belonging as a design requirement — who feels invited is part of the mission, so copy and imagery must widen the door, especially for under-resourced students. Be credible, not corporate — earn trust through substance and Stanford grounding rather than gloss. Keep a human voice over institutional polish — plainspoken and warm beats formal and impressive.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, and a reduced-motion alternative for the dot-scatter, trajectory draw, and scroll reveals (already partially implemented in `globals.css`). Given the equity mission, accessibility is treated as core rather than compliance — legible type sizes and never conveying meaning through color alone.
